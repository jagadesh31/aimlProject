Continuous Speech Emotion Recognition with Temporal Emotion Tracking

Project goal: detect emotion from voice (audio), continuously over time, and show a readable timeline of stable emotions and transitions. Main dataset: MELD.

1. How to position your project

Do not sell this as “best accuracy on MELD.” Sell it as:

Positioning line:
“Most SER systems give one emotion for one clip. In conversation, emotion changes. We build a continuous, audio-only pipeline that predicts emotion on sliding windows and converts those predictions into an interpretable timeline of stable regions and transitions.”
Do sayDon’t say
Continuous emotion tracking on speech We read emotion from text

Novelty is the timeline module We invented CNN + BiLSTM

2. What problem are we solving?

Normal SER

One short audio clip → one emotion label (happy / angry / sad…).

Real conversation problem

In a dialogue, mood can stay for a few seconds, then change. One label for the whole recording hides when the change happened. Dumping every window prediction is noisy and hard for a human to read.

Our answer

  1. Predict emotion every 1 second using 2-second audio windows.
  2. Smooth weak / jumping predictions.
  3. Group same emotions into stable regions.
  4. Insert short transition markers when emotion changes.
  5. Print a clean timeline.
Say this: “Continuous SER here means emotion over time from speech audio, not one label for the full file, and not emotion from text.”

3. Novelty and contribution (present as FOUR parts)

Do not reduce the project to “we smoothed labels.” Present an end-to-end continuous SER system.

  1. MELD audio pipeline — CSV labels mapped to dia*_utt* WAVs; audio-only SER.
  2. Acoustic model — log-mel+deltas → CNN+BiLSTM; utterance accuracy improved ~34%→~49%.
  3. Continuous recognition design — 2 s / 1 s windows over stitched dialogue (method, not a side script).
  4. Temporal structure + continuous eval — stable/transition timeline; window-majority scored vs gold turns.
Say this: “Our contribution is a full continuous speech emotion recognition stack on conversational MELD audio. Temporal tracking is one module; the project also includes data prep, acoustic modeling, dense window inference, and dual evaluation.”

4. Dataset — MELD (main)

MELD = Multimodal EmotionLines Dataset (Poria et al., ACL 2019). Dialogues from the TV show Friends, multi-party conversations.

ItemValue
Dialogues~1433
Utterances> 13,000
Train / Dev / Test~9989 / ~1109 / ~2610
Emotions (7)anger, disgust, fear, joy, neutral, sadness, surprise
What we useAudio only (MP4 → WAV). Labels for train/eval.
What we do not useText and video modalities

What “emotion changes across turns” means

A dialogue is one scene. A turn / utterance is one spoken line. Each turn has its own emotion label. From turn to turn, the label can change (neutral → anger → surprise). That is emotion changing across turns.

Why not the “continuous” datasets we discussed?

Some continuous-affect datasets need registration / college permission / research agreements (examples often discussed: IEMOCAP license, RECOLA, SEWA). We did not have that access. MELD is public and conversational, so we used MELD and built continuous tracking ourselves.

RAVDESS (only early testing)

Acted speech, one emotion per clip, not conversation. Used only to test the pipeline while MELD was downloading. For review: dataset = MELD.

6. Mel spectrogram, CNN, BiLSTM (explain simply)

Is this voice or text?

Voice. We never feed dialogue text into the model.

What is a mel spectrogram?

Yes — it converts audio into frequency over time. X-axis = time, Y-axis = frequency bands (mel scale, closer to human hearing), intensity = energy. The model “sees” this like an image.

Delta / delta-delta

How the spectrum changes over time. Helps separate emotions that may look similar in a static average (e.g. joy vs neutral).

CNN

Finds local spectrogram patterns (bursts, pitch bands, roughness).

BiLSTM

Reads the sequence forward and backward so emotion that unfolds across the utterance can use both sides of the clip.

7. Temporal tracking (the novel part)

StepWhat happens
Window detect2 s window, 1 s hop → emotion + confidence
SmoothLocal majority; kill isolated low-confidence flips (conf < 0.35)
Stable regionSame emotion lasting as one interval
Transition0.5 s marker: old emotion → new emotion
OutputHuman-readable timeline + JSON

Important: transition is a rule, not a class learned by the network. MELD gold labels are still per utterance, not per half-second. 

Example timeline style:
  0.0–4.0 s   Neutral (stable)
  4.0–4.5 s   Transition: neutral -> anger
  4.5–8.0 s   Anger (stable)

12. Likely viva questions (short answers)

Q. Continuous SER — voice or text?
Voice / speech audio only.

Q. Mel spectrogram?
Time–frequency image of speech energy on mel scale.

Q. Dataset?
MELD. RAVDESS only for early pipeline test.

Q. Novelty?
temporal_tracking: smooth + stable + transitions → timeline.

Q. Why accuracy ~49%?
Audio-only MELD is hard; Neutral ≈48% of test. We beat our old 34% run; minority emotions remain hard.

Q. Is timeline ground truth?
No. Ground truth is utterance labels. Timeline is an interpretable layer.

Q. Emotion changes across turns?
Each spoken line has its own label; labels can differ from turn to turn in a dialogue.

“Ma’am, our project is Continuous Speech Emotion Recognition. Normal SER gives one emotion for one clip. In conversation, emotion changes over time. We use the MELD dataset — Friends dialogues with seven emotions — and we use only audio. First we convert speech to a mel spectrogram with delta features, then a CNN–BiLSTM predicts the emotion. For continuous tracking, we slide a 2-second window every 1 second, smooth weak jumps, and build a timeline of stable emotions and short transitions. That timeline module is our contribution; CNN and BiLSTM are standard support. On MELD test we currently get about 49% accuracy and 0.36 weighted F1, improved from our earlier 34% run. Audio-only MELD is difficult because Neutral dominates. Our aim is interpretable continuous tracking, not multimodal SOTA. I can show the dialogue timeline demo and the block-diagram PDF.”
